## Part 1: The Abu Bakr (RA) Compilation: Gathering the Written Sheets (Suhuf)

The Catalyst: After the Battle of Yamama (during the Riddah Wars), scores of companion Huffadh (those who had memorized the entire Qur'an word-for-word) were killed. Umar ibn al-Khattab feared that if more memorizers were lost in battles, parts of the oral memory could become vulnerable.

The Task Assigned to Zayd ibn Thabit (RA): Zayd was chosen because he was a primary scribe of the Prophet ﷺ, had memorized the complete text himself, and was recognized for his scrupulous character and intellect.

The Dual-Witness Standard: Zayd did not accept any verse based on memory alone, nor on isolated written fragments. Every written piece brought to him had to be accompanied by two independent witnesses testifying that the fragment was inscribed directly in the presence of the Prophet ﷺ during his lifetime, and it had to match the unbroken oral memorization of the surviving companions.

## Part 2: The Uthman (RA) Standardization: Dialectical Unity (The Mushaf al-Imam)

The Custody of the Suhuf: The bound collection was kept with Abu Bakr, passed to Umar upon his succession, and after Umar's death, entrusted to his daughter Hafsa (RA), a widow of the Prophet ﷺ who had also memorized the text.

By the reign of the third Caliph, Uthman ibn Affan, the Islamic empire had expanded rapidly across Syria, Iraq, Persia, and the Caucasus. This geographic spread triggered a specific crisis:

The Problem Observed by Hudhayfah ibn al-Yaman: While campaigning in Armenia and Azerbaijan, the commander Hudhayfah noticed soldiers from Kufa and Damascus arguing over differences in pronunciation and dialect during communal recitation. Some were declaring another's recitation invalid. Hudhayfah rushed back to Medina and warned Uthman: "Save this Ummah before they differ regarding their Book as the Jews and Christians differed."

The Seven Ahruf (Dialects): The Prophet ﷺ had received permission for the Qur'an to be recited in seven tribal dialectical variations (ahruf) to accommodate the varied Arabic tribes of the peninsula. However, as non-Arabs and distant communities entered Islam, these variations were causing confusion and sectarian division.

Forming the Committee: Uthman requested the original master copy (Suhuf) from Hafsa. He then appointed a committee led by Zayd ibn Thabit alongside three Qurayshi scholars (Abdullah ibn al-Zubayr, Sa'id ibn al-'As, and Abd al-Rahman ibn al-Harith). Uthman instructed them: "If you disagree with Zayd on any point of phrasing, write it in the dialect of Quraysh, for it was revealed in their tongue."

The Rasm (Consonantal Skeleton): The committee produced standardized copies (Mushaf al-Imam) written in an unpointed consonantal script that preserved the authentic reading of the final review (al-'Ardah al-Akhirah) conducted by the Prophet ﷺ before his death, while still accommodating valid canonical recitations (Qira'at).
